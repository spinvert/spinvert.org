The board consists of an active rectifier, a DC/DC boost converter with bypass, a dump load
output and the measurement and control circuitry. The schematic and PCB are designed with KiCad,
and the design files are available in the
[GitHub repository](https://github.com/spinvert/spinvert-boost-4820).

## Active rectifier

The rectifier is a 3-phase bridge with six MOSFETs, two per phase. The MOSFETs are switched on
while the corresponding diode of a diode bridge would conduct, so that the current flows through
the low channel resistance instead of a diode. The rectifier is rated for 20 A. See
[Rectifiers](/theory/rectifiers/#active-rectifier) for the operating principle and a comparison
of the losses with a diode bridge.

The six MOSFETs (Infineon BSC040N10NS5, 100 V, 4 mΩ) are controlled by two LM74680 ideal diode
bridge controllers from Texas Instruments. They measure the voltage across each MOSFET and switch
it on whenever its body diode would conduct, without any involvement of the microcontroller.

## Boost converter

The boost converter steps the rectified voltage up to the battery voltage. It uses a synchronous
topology with two MOSFETs: the low-side switch builds up the inductor current, and the high-side
switch replaces the diode of a basic boost converter. Both are of the same type as in the
rectifier and are driven by an LM5107 half-bridge driver. The inductor current is measured with a
shunt resistor and an INA299 current sense amplifier. The operating principle is described in
[DC/DC converters](/theory/converters/#boost-converter).

| Parameter                | Value                    |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Inductance $L$           | 47 µH                    |
| Switching frequency      | 128 kHz                  |
| Max. output current      | 10 A (480 W at 48 V)     |

With these values, the inductor current ripple reaches its maximum of
$\Delta I_{L,max} = V_{out} / (4 \, L \, f_{sw}) \approx 2\ \text{A}$ at $V_{in} = V_{out}/2$.
At low input voltages, the input current $I_{in} = P / V_{in}$ becomes high, so the power is
also limited by the current rating at the input.

The high switching frequency allows a small inductor, which is available as an off-the-shelf
component. The input and output of the converter are filtered to meet EMC requirements.

## Bypass

The bypass is a Schottky diode (SDT30B100D1, 100 V, 30 A) from the rectifier output to the
battery. When the rectified voltage reaches the battery voltage, the duty cycle of the boost
converter approaches zero and boosting is no longer possible. As soon as the rectified voltage
exceeds the battery voltage by the forward voltage of the diode, the diode conducts
automatically and passes the current directly to the battery, without any control by the
microcontroller. The bypass is rated for 20 A. Its losses are the conduction losses of the diode,
i.e. its forward voltage of around 0.6 V times the current.

In bypass mode, the battery voltage determines the rectified voltage. The current is then given
by the generator voltage, the battery voltage and the internal resistance of the generator, as
described in [Operating curve](/hardware/boost-4820/operating-curve/#limits-of-the-board).

## Dump load

The dump load output is connected to the rectified voltage, in parallel to the boost converter
and the bypass. It can carry up to 20 A. Any resistive load can be used, e.g. heating elements
for water or air.

The resistance of the dump load determines how much power it can absorb at a given voltage:

$$
I = \frac{V_{dc}}{R_{dump}}
\qquad
P = \frac{V_{dc}^2}{R_{dump}}
$$

The resistance should be chosen such that the current stays below 20 A at the maximum input
voltage of 60 V, i.e. $R_{dump} \geq 3\ \Omega$. A 3 Ω load absorbs 768 W at 48 V and 1200 W
at 60 V, so its power rating must be sufficient for continuous operation.

The dump load is connected between the terminals D+ (rectified voltage) and D−, which is
switched to ground by a MOSFET. A free-wheeling diode across the terminals takes over the current
of inductive loads when the MOSFET turns off.

## Measurements

The control of the board is based on the following measurements:

- **AC voltages** of the generator phases, used to determine the electrical frequency and thus
  the speed of the generator
- **Rectified voltage** at the input of the boost converter
- **Boost converter input current**, controlled according to the lookup curve

The measurements and the control are handled by an STM32G0B1 microcontroller.

The speed is calculated from the frequency as $n = 2 f_e / p$ (in s⁻¹, multiplied by 60 s/min
for rpm), which requires the pole count $p$ of the generator to be configured.

## Connectors

| Connector | Type                               | Pins                                       |
| --------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| J3        | Screw terminal, 3-pin, 9.5 mm      | Generator phases L1, L2, L3                |
| J6        | Screw terminal, 2-pin, 9.5 mm      | Dump load D+, D−                           |
| J2        | Screw terminal, 2-pin, 9.5 mm      | Battery B−, B+                             |
| J1        | Pluggable terminal, 3-pin, 3.81 mm | CAN: GND, CAN_L, CAN_H                     |
| J4        | USB-C (USB 2.0)                    | Monitoring and debugging                   |
| J5        | Pin header, 7-pin, 2.54 mm         | 3V3, SWCLK, GND, SWDIO, RST, TX, RX        |

The power terminals are located along one edge of the board. The pin header J5 provides the SWD
interface for programming the microcontroller and a serial console (UART).

The board measures 90 × 90 mm and has four M3 mounting holes with a spacing of 82 × 82 mm.
