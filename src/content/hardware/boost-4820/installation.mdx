## System overview

A typical system consists of the following components:

- **Wind turbine** (or other generator) with a 3-phase permanent magnet generator
- **Stop switch** that shorts the generator phases to brake the turbine
- **Spinvert Boost** board
- **Dump load**, e.g. a heating element, connected to the dump load output
- **48 V battery** with fuse and, ideally, a battery management system
- Loads or an **off-grid inverter** connected to the battery

## Safety

**Never leave the turbine without load.** A wind turbine without load accelerates to its runaway
speed. Besides the mechanical stress, the generator voltage rises with the speed and can exceed
the input voltage rating of 60 V. Make sure that a dump load is always connected and that the
turbine can be stopped independent of the electronics.

**Overspeed protection:** The dump load of the Spinvert Boost is not a replacement for the
turbine's own protection against high wind speeds. The turbine needs a secondary protection that
works without the board, e.g. furling or a separate automatically activated electrical brake.

**Stop switch:** Shorting the three phases of a permanent magnet generator brakes the rotor
electrically. Install a switch rated for the short-circuit current of the generator between the
turbine and the board, so that the turbine can be stopped before working on the system. Activate
the switch at low wind speeds only, as the braking at high speeds causes high currents and
mechanical loads.

**Battery fuse:** Install a fuse or circuit breaker as close as possible to the positive battery
terminal. Batteries can deliver very high short-circuit currents.

**Cables:** Size the cables for the maximum current of 20 A and keep the voltage drop low,
especially between generator and board. At low wind speeds, the generator voltage is only a few
volts, so a voltage drop of 1 V already causes a significant loss.

**Lightning and grounding:** Wind turbine towers are exposed to lightning. Ground the tower and
consider surge protection on the cables from the turbine.

## Commissioning

1. Determine the generator parameters, either from the data sheet or by
   [measurement](/theory/generators/#measuring-your-generator).
2. Calculate the lookup curve with the [calculator](/hardware/boost-4820/operating-curve/#calculator). Check
   that the input voltage rating is respected and that the dump load can absorb the expected
   power.
3. Install the system with the stop switch activated.
4. Connect the battery and check the configuration via USB.
5. Release the stop switch at low wind speeds and monitor the operating mode, speed, voltages and
   currents.
6. Compare the measured operating points with the values from the calculator. If the rotor runs
   consistently faster or slower than expected, adjust $C_{p,max}$ and $\lambda_{opt}$ and
   recalculate the curve.
