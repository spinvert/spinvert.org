This section explains the background behind the Spinvert hardware: how small wind turbines, micro
hydro turbines and pedal generators convert mechanical power to electricity, and how this power
is transferred to a battery as efficiently as possible. The content is independent of a specific
product. For the Spinvert Boost 4820, see the [hardware documentation](/hardware/boost-4820/).

## From rotation to battery charge

A small renewable energy system with a rotating source consists of the following chain:

1. **Mechanical source:** A wind rotor, water turbine or pedal crank delivers mechanical power.
   Its power depends on the speed in a characteristic way, with an optimum at a certain speed.
2. **Generator:** A 3-phase permanent magnet generator converts the mechanical power to AC
   with a voltage and frequency proportional to the speed.
3. **Rectifier:** The 3-phase AC is converted to DC.
4. **DC/DC converter:** The rectified voltage is matched to the battery voltage. At the same
   time, the converter controls the current drawn from the generator and thus the load on the
   source.
5. **Battery and loads:** The energy is stored in a battery or used directly, e.g. via an
   off-grid inverter.

Each stage causes losses, and the stages influence each other. For example, the generator
voltage determines which converter topology is suitable, and the converter determines whether
the source can be operated at its optimum.
