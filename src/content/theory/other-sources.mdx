The concept of a lookup curve over speed described in
[Optimal operation](/theory/optimal-operation/#tracking-methods) is not limited to wind turbines.
Only the source of the curve is different.

## Micro hydro

The power available from water is $P = \rho g Q H \eta$ with flow rate $Q$, head $H$ and
turbine efficiency $\eta$. Impulse turbines such as Pelton wheels reach their maximum efficiency
when the runner speed is about half the jet velocity $v_{jet} = \sqrt{2 g H}$. As the head is
usually constant, the optimal speed is almost constant, too. The lookup curve becomes very steep
around the optimal speed, so that the turbine is held at this speed independent of the flow
rate.

## Pedal power

Here, the curve defines how pedaling feels. A power curve rising with the cube of the speed (like
the one of a wind turbine) feels similar to riding a bicycle on a flat road, where the air drag
dominates. Cyclists are most efficient at cadences between 60 and 90 rpm, so the gear ratio and
the curve should be chosen such that the desired power is reached in this range.
