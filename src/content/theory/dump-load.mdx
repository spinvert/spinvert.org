---
title: Dump load
description: Why a wind turbine needs a dump load, where it can be connected, how it is controlled and sized.

---

A solar charge controller can simply stop charging when the battery is full: the panels then
just sit at their open-circuit voltage. A wind turbine can't be treated this way. Without load,
the rotor accelerates to its runaway speed, typically 1.5 to 2 times the optimal speed. The
mechanical stress increases, and the generator voltage rises proportionally to the speed.

A dump load (also called diversion load) takes over the power whenever it can't be used
otherwise, e.g. when the battery is full or disconnected. It is usually a resistive heating
element, so the excess energy can still be used to heat water or air.

## Connection options

**Battery side:** In classic small wind systems with a direct battery connection, a diversion
controller connects the dump load to the battery when the battery voltage gets too high. This is
simple, but it only works as long as the battery is connected. If a fuse blows or a cable comes
loose, the turbine runs without load.

**Rectifier side:** The dump load is connected directly to the rectified generator voltage,
before any converter. It can load the generator independent of the battery and of the converter,
and it can also be used to limit the generator voltage. This requires a switch rated for the
full generator current.

**AC side:** Three resistors are connected to the generator phases. This avoids the rectifier, but
needs three switches and is rarely used for small turbines.

## Control

The simplest control switches the dump load on when a voltage threshold is exceeded and off again
below a lower threshold (hysteresis). This causes the generator load, and thus the rotor speed,
to jump between two states.

With pulse width modulation (PWM), the dump load is switched on and off quickly, and the duty
cycle sets the average power. This allows the controller to divert exactly the power that is not
needed, so the turbine can stay at its optimal operating point even when the battery is full.

## Sizing

The resistance $R$ of the dump load determines the current and power at a given voltage $V$:

$$
I = \frac{V}{R}
\qquad
P = \frac{V^2}{R}
$$

Two conditions have to be met:

- The resistance has to be low enough to absorb the maximum power of the turbine at the voltage
  where the dump load operates.
- It has to be high enough that the current stays within the rating of the switch, even at the
  highest voltage.

The dump load must be rated for continuous operation at full power, as it may run for hours
during a windy period with a full battery.

## Dump load is not a brake

A dump load keeps the turbine loaded in normal operation, but it can't protect the turbine in a
storm. Above its rated wind speed, a turbine has to limit its power by mechanical means, e.g. by
furling (turning the rotor out of the wind). For maintenance or emergencies, a stop switch that
shorts the generator phases can brake the turbine, but this should only be used at low wind
speeds, as braking at high speeds causes very high currents and mechanical loads.
